How to read the model

What students should notice

  • The half-cell with the more positive reduction potential is the cathode.
  • Oxidation occurs at the anode; electrons leave it through the wire.
  • Salt-bridge cations migrate toward the cathode and anions toward the anode to maintain electrical neutrality.
  • Changing concentration changes E, not the tabulated E°.

Assumptions

  • Concentration is used as an approximation to activity.
  • Pure solids have unit activity and gases behave ideally.
  • Liquid-junction potential, internal resistance and kinetic overpotential are not modeled.
  • The displayed EMF is the reversible, open-circuit value.
  • Na, Ca and K couples are reference data only: those metals react with water, so the pictured aqueous half-cell is not experimentally viable.

Good discussion prompt

“If the voltmeter reads 1.10 V, will this lamp definitely glow?”

No. Voltage is the energy transferred per coulomb. It does not tell us how many coulombs per second the cell can deliver.

Sign convention

Ecell = Ecathode − Eanode, using reduction potentials for both. The simulation always reports the spontaneous direction, so the magnitude is non-negative.
